# P9 research decision

**Recommendation: submit as a methods paper on evaluating mathematical verifiers.** The contribution is narrow enough to test. Given two fixed score vectors, it connects the answer-level decision to the best attainable uncertainty width under missing grades, a bounded number of faulty reviews, and a choice between two adjudication costs. The shared-answer and whole-question analyses make the unit of review explicit. The paper demonstrates both a favorable case for selective review and cases in which checking every ordinary label still cannot settle a close comparison at the stated error allowance.

The portfolio initially proposed a proposer–critic or debate benchmark, with stopping, correlated errors and compute budgets as possible questions. There was no P9 manuscript or experiment in the active or archived project trees that we inspected. We retained mathematical verification and budgeted decisions and chose a question answerable with paired public score releases. The final comparison uses fixed, previously scored solutions.

## Novelty assessment

Choosing informative judgments is established in retrieval evaluation. Carterette et al. study minimal test collections. Tan and Clarke bound effectiveness differences under partial judgments. Rank-based best-of-N evaluation and probabilistic active model comparison also have substantial prior work. The paper credits these results. The proposed contribution consists of the sharp two-tier allocation with fallible ordinary reviews, its application after cancellation of shared mathematical answers, and the empirical contrast between certification effort and vulnerability to adverse grading changes. The sources checked are listed in the manuscript. This is a scoped novelty assessment, with no claim of an exhaustive priority search.

## Interpretation for review

The strongest claim concerns the evaluated archive and a specified sampling policy. The proofs, exhaustive small-instance checks and pinned replay all address that claim. The two data releases share MATH-500 questions and should not be counted as independent problem samples. The changed-label radius describes mathematical sensitivity. No one regraded the indicated answers or measured human review time. The cost ratios and the reliability of the gold tier are explicit assumptions. A journal seeking a prospective human annotation study or a new reasoning model would be evaluating a different claim.

## Decision after the second review

The narrow methods claim warrants submission. The previous assessment gave too much weight to the clean-label review savings and too little to whether an auditor could justify the assumed count of errors. Three comparisons define the contribution.

- The clean linear interval with shared item grades follows directly from Tan and Clarke's maximized effectiveness difference. The clean priority follows by calculating the width reduction from revealing one grade. Targeted rank judgments and exposure-difference sampling have long precedents. The finite-pool winner formula also appears explicitly in prior best-of-N work. [Tan and Clarke](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.3587), [Moffat et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/1277741.1277806), [Schnabel et al.](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~adith/docs/UBCompEval.pdf), [Jacobson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07453)
- A single endpoint uses standard budgeted linear uncertainty. The additional result is the sharp two-sided diameter under one shared, fallible transcript, plus the optimal fixed-price allocation. After provisional grades arrive, we also derive the optimal allocation of a fixed number of authoritative upgrades across the two correction directions, including a lower bound for deterministic sequential strategies. The guarantee concerns worst remaining width. [Bertsimas and Sim](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1030.0065)
- Estimating a correction to provisional grades from authoritative observations has direct statistical precedents. The matched-budget stratified sampling analysis is an adversarial comparator for the proposed design. [Angelopoulos et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09633)

The strength of the evidence depends on which claim is being made. Exhaustive small-instance checks and proofs support the minimax characterization. At the original fixed allowance of five errors, mixing tiers strictly improves on the better pure policy in 38 of 96 grid cells. At full Lightman depth, both operational graders violate that allowance and both five-error intervals miss the archived 5.8-point gap. Uniform calibration and conditional upgrades leave all six primary signs unresolved. Direct probability sampling has a smaller median width in every cell over 32 seeds. Its full-depth rational-grader comparison resolves 12 of 32 signs versus five for conditional upgrades. There is no demonstrated general efficiency advantage when the error ceiling must be estimated. These trials use two related string-based graders on one archive. Human grade quality, actual review time and generality to new model pairs remain unmeasured.

**Recommended journal: PLOS ONE, as an original computational methods study.** Its stated criteria emphasize a technically sound analysis, reproducibility and conclusions supported by the data. The practical point a reader can reuse is how to distinguish a worst-case guarantee with a defensible error allowance from a statistical audit that learns about grader errors. The candid comparison showing when direct sampling works better is important to that case. The manuscript discloses which aspects of the study used Codex. The named authors should independently check the claims and approve the manuscript and disclosure before submission.
